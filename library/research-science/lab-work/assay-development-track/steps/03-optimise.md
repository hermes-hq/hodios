# Step 3: Optimise

Tune the factors that matter, with as few runs as possible.

1. List the candidate factors (for example reagent concentrations, incubation times and temperatures, sample dilution, blocking, wash steps) and rank them by expected effect from the pilot.
2. Choose the approach: one factor at a time for two or three factors, or a screening design of experiments followed by a response surface for more, with the reason. Lay out the runs, including centre points and replicates, and suggest plate layouts where relevant.
3. Define the response to optimise (signal-to-background, a robustness measure such as Z-prime for screening assays, precision) and the trade-offs.
4. Ask for the results, then interpret them, pick the operating conditions and note any factor the assay is sensitive to, which becomes a robustness check in validation.

Stop and wait for approval of the final conditions.
