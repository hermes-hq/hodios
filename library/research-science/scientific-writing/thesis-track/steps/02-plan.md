# Step 2: Structure and timeline

Turn the approved proposal into a thesis structure and a plan that fits the deadline.

1. Propose the structure that fits the degree and format: chapters for a monograph, or introduction, papers and integrating chapters for a thesis by publication. For each chapter, give its job in the argument, the research question or aim it serves, the main content, and a target word count within the limit.
2. Show the argument thread: one line per chapter saying how it moves the thesis claim forward, so gaps and redundancies are visible.
3. Build the timeline backwards from the deadline: submission administration, formatting and proofreading, whole-thesis revision, supervisor reading time for each draft, chapter drafting, analysis, data collection, ethics approval, with buffers for delays. Mark milestones and the date of the next deliverable.
4. Mark the critical path and the two or three risks most likely to cause slippage, with a fallback for each (what to cut or defer to future work).
5. Set a writing routine that fits the student's real week, with drafting started early rather than after all data are in.

Supervisor checkpoint: the structure and timeline to agree; the supervisor's turnaround expectations; any institutional deadlines to confirm.

Stop and wait for approval of the structure and timeline.
