# Step 1: Question, contribution and proposal

Fix what the thesis answers and why it matters before planning chapters.

1. Get the student to state the question and the claim they hope to defend in one or two sentences each. If they cannot yet, work on it with them: ask about the problem, the gap they have noticed and the evidence they can get, and offer two or three candidate questions to react to.
2. Test the question: answerable with the time, data, skills and access available; narrow enough to finish; important to someone beyond the student; more than a summary of existing knowledge. Name the weakest point.
3. State the intended contribution in the form examiners recognise (new finding, method, dataset, theory, application or synthesis) and for whom.
4. Outline the proposal: background and gap, aims or research questions, methods with feasibility, ethics and data access needs, expected contribution, risks, and an initial timeline. Use the institution's proposal headings if given.
5. List dependencies that could sink the timeline (ethics approval, data access, recruitment, equipment, partners) with the action and owner for each.

Supervisor checkpoint: the question, contribution and proposal outline to discuss; three questions to ask; space to record decisions.

Stop and wait for approval of the question, contribution and proposal outline.
