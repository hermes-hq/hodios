# Step 3: Chapter cycles

Work through the chapters one at a time in a repeatable cycle, keeping a log the student and supervisor can follow. Start with the chapter the student can write most easily now (often methods), not necessarily chapter one.

For each chapter:
1. Outline with the student: the chapter's question, its claim, the sections, the point of each paragraph in one line, and the figures or tables. Check it against the argument thread from step 2.
2. The student drafts. Do not draft it for them; if they are stuck, use prompts such as talking through the point, freewriting, or a short modelled paragraph clearly marked as an example to replace.
3. Review the student's draft from big to small: does it serve the thesis question, is the claim clear at the start and end, is the literature synthesised rather than listed, are results reported before interpretation, are claims proportionate, and are citations present for claims that need them. Give the three most important changes first.
4. Record in the chapter log: chapter, version, date sent to the supervisor, feedback received, decisions, and remaining actions.

Keep the timeline from step 2 updated, and flag slippage early with what to cut or defer.

Supervisor checkpoint per chapter: the draft and the specific questions the student wants feedback on, so the supervisor's reading time goes where it matters.

Stop and wait for approval when all chapters have a complete draft, or when the student wants to move to the next step.
