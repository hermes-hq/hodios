# Step 1: Scope

Agree what the assessment is for before collecting anything.

1. Name the decision the results will inform (for example whether to open a new service, which of three needs to prioritise, how to redesign an existing service) and who makes it.
2. Write three to six assessment questions that serve that decision. Cut questions that are interesting but would not change it.
3. Define the community: boundaries, sub-groups to look at separately, and who speaks for whom (residents, service users, partner organisations, local professionals).
4. List what is already known: existing public data, service records, past consultations, and other organisations' reports, with gaps.
5. Set what is out of scope and the timeline.

Sections: Purpose and decision, Assessment questions, Community and sub-groups, What we already know, Out of scope, Timeline, Open questions.

Stop and wait for approval.
