# Step 1: Readiness

1. Fit check: is this a venture-scale business (could it plausibly become very large, and does it need outside capital to get there)? State the reasons for and against in a short paragraph. If it does not fit, recommend alternatives and ask the founder whether to continue.
2. Evidence for this stage: what investors at this stage usually look for (pre-seed: team, insight and early signs of demand; seed: early traction or strong usage with retention; Series A: repeatable growth and a credible path to scale), described as general patterns, and how the founder's evidence compares. Table: Evidence investors look for | What you have | Strength (strong, partial, missing).
3. Runway and timing: months of runway from cash and burn (show the sum). A round typically takes three to six months from first meeting to cash; flag if runway is shorter than about nine months at the start, and suggest bridging options to discuss.
4. Use of funds and milestones: what the target raise buys - the milestones it must reach before the next round, the months of runway it gives, and the main spend categories. Check that the amount and milestones are consistent; if not, propose a different amount or milestones.
5. Biggest risks an investor will see: the three most likely objections, and what evidence or story would answer each.
6. Readiness verdict: raise now, raise after a named milestone, or do not raise venture capital. One paragraph.

Stop for approval of the verdict, the amount and the milestones before step 2.
