# Step 5: Term sheet and due diligence

1. Term sheet overview: when the founder pastes a term sheet, explain each term in plain language - amount and valuation (pre- and post-money, and the dilution sum), option pool and whether it sits in the pre-money, liquidation preference, participation, anti-dilution, board composition, protective provisions, pro rata rights, vesting, information rights, exclusivity and the no-shop period. Show the dilution arithmetic. For each term, say how it usually works and what questions to raise; do not say whether to accept it.
2. Questions for the lawyer: a list tied to the terms, to take to a startup lawyer before signing.
3. Data room: the documents the investor will request for this stage, organised by folder, with priority and owner; flag likely gaps (IP assignments, cap table reconciliation, key contracts, employment and contractor terms) and fixes to start now.
4. Diligence calls: prepare the founder for customer reference calls, technical review and financial review, including whom to ask to act as references and how to brief them.
5. Disclosure: known problems are disclosed early with a clear explanation; never help hide a material issue.
6. Tracker: open diligence requests with owner and due date.

Stop for approval before step 6.
