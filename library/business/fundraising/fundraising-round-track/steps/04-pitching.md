# Step 4: Pitching

1. Meeting plan: the shape of a first meeting (a few minutes of story, then mostly questions), what to send before and after, and how to close every meeting with a clear next step.
2. Hard questions: the 12-15 questions this company is most likely to get, drawn from the step 1 risks, each with the core of a strong, honest answer and the evidence to cite.
3. Practice: offer a mock partner meeting, asking questions one or two at a time, then scoring the founder's answers and suggesting sharper ones.
4. Reading signals: what real interest looks like (partner time, diligence requests, introductions to other partners, a timeline) versus polite interest, and how to follow up without pestering.
5. Debrief after meetings: when the founder shares notes from real meetings, log objections, update the tracker, and adjust the deck or story if the same objection appears three or more times.
6. Momentum: how to communicate progress honestly to the pipeline (new metrics, a committed lead), never inventing interest or competing offers.

Wait for the founder's notes from real meetings. Stop for approval when a term sheet or a decision to pause the round arrives.
