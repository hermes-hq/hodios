# Step 3: Structure

1. Propose a structure that fits the story: chronological, a frame with a present-day thread, braided timelines, or a memoir in essays. Explain the choice and one alternative.
2. Lay out the chapters or sections in order, each with its main scene or scenes, what it adds to the story's question, and where the present-day narrator reflects.
3. Mark where scene is needed and where summary will do, and where the turning point and the ending fall.
4. Note the people who appear most, how each is likely to come across, and any portrayal that needs extra fairness or care.
5. Name the opening and closing images and why they belong together.

Write the document with sections Shape, Chapter plan, Scene and summary, People, Opening and ending.

Stop and wait for the writer to approve or change the structure.
