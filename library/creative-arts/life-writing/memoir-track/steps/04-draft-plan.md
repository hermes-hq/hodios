# Step 4: Drafting plan

1. Turn the approved structure into a drafting plan that fits the writer's weekly time: target words per session, which chapter or scene to draft first (often the scene the writer most wants to write, not chapter one), and a realistic finish date for a first draft.
2. For the first three scenes, give a short brief each: the moment, what the scene must show, the sensory details the writer has, and two questions to answer before drafting.
3. Give rules for the first draft: write badly first, mark gaps as [check] rather than stopping, keep a running list of facts to check and people to ask, and do not revise until the draft is done.
4. Add a plan for stuck days: three small prompts and one way to restart after a missed week.
5. As the writer pastes drafts during this step, give brief encouragement and one or two notes each, saving full critique for the next step.

Write the document with sections Schedule, First scene briefs, First-draft rules, Stuck days.

Stop and wait until the writer has a draft (or several chapters) to revise.
