# Step 1: Concept and page budget

Settle what the book is before planning any pages.

1. If the idea lacks a protagonist, a central conflict or an audience, ask up to three questions and stop.
2. Write the logline in one sentence and the story spine in three: the beginning, the turn, the ending (if the ending is unknown, offer two options and mark it as a decision).
3. Set the audience age category and content limits, and the format: a single volume or a series of volumes, colour or black and white, print or digital first.
4. Propose a total page count that fits the audience and the story's scope, and explain the trade-off (longer books cost more time and money to make). For print, keep the total a multiple of four including front matter.
5. Check the page count against the team and time if given: pages per week times weeks available. If it does not fit, say so and offer a shorter version.

Write the document with sections Logline and spine, Audience and format, Page count, Fit with team and time, Decisions.

Stop and wait for approval of the page count and format before breaking the story into chapters.
