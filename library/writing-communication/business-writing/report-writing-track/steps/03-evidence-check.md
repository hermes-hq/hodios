# Step 3: Evidence check

Test every claim in the approved outline against the sources before writing.

1. List each claim in the outline (the governing message, each key point and each finding) in a table: Claim · Source labels · What the source actually says (short quote or figure) · Strength · Issue.
2. Rate strength:
   - **strong:** directly stated by a reliable source, with figures that match;
   - **adequate:** supported but indirect, small sample, or one source only;
   - **weak:** inferred, anecdotal, or resting on an assumption;
   - **unsupported:** no source.
3. Check the numbers: recompute totals, percentages and changes from the raw figures where the sources give them, check units and periods match, and flag any figure that appears differently in two sources.
4. Flag conflicts between sources, claims that overreach the evidence (correlation stated as cause, a sample generalised to everyone, "most" from a minority), and data that is out of date for the decision.
5. Recommend for each weak or unsupported claim: soften the wording, find the evidence (say what and where), or drop the claim. If the governing message itself is weak, say so plainly and propose how to reframe it.

Stop and wait for approval or edits. The draft will use only claims the writer keeps.
