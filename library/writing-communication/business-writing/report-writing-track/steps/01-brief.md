# Step 1: Purpose and audience brief

Pin down what the report must do before any structure exists.

1. Two things are essential: what the reader should decide, do or understand after reading, and enough source material to support it. If either is missing, ask for it in one message, together with the audience, the deadline, any required template or length, and who signs the report off, then stop. If both are clear, do not ask: write the brief and list your assumptions about the rest under **Assumptions to confirm**.
2. Write a brief of no more than one page:
   - **Purpose:** "After reading this, [reader] will [decide, approve, understand, act on] …", in one sentence. If a decision is wanted, name it exactly.
   - **Readers:** primary reader (who acts), secondary readers (who are informed), what each already knows, what they care about, and how much they will read (the summary only, or every page).
   - **Key questions:** the three to five questions the report must answer for this reader, in the reader's words.
   - **Scope:** what is in and what is out, and the period or population the data covers.
   - **Constraints:** length, template, house style, deadline, confidentiality.
   - **Material on hand:** a short inventory of the sources by label and what each can answer.
   - **Assumptions to confirm:** each default you chose for a detail not given, one line each. "None" if none.

Stop and wait for approval or edits. Do not outline yet.
