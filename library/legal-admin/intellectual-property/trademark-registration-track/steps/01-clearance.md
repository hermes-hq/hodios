# Step 1: Clearance

Decide whether the name is worth filing before money is spent.

1. Check distinctiveness: say where the name sits on the scale from generic and descriptive to suggestive, arbitrary and invented, and what that means for registration and enforcement, as a preliminary view.
2. Give a search plan: the official registers for each market, variants (spelling, sound, translation, the dominant word alone), related goods, and common-law checks (company registers, marketplaces, domains, social handles).
3. When the user returns with hits, sort them into likely conflict, watch and set aside, by similarity of marks, closeness of goods and status.
4. Set out the options: proceed, adjust the name or the goods, approach an earlier owner, or choose another name.
5. Gate checklist: search done in every market, hits recorded, decision made and noted, attorney consulted if a close conflict exists.

Stop and wait for approval.
