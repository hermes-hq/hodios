# Step 3: Go or no-go

Make the call the day before, with the real forecast and how everyone is.

1. Ask for or read the forecast they paste, for the height and exposure of the route: wind on exposed ground, temperatures and wind chill at the high point, freezing level, precipitation timing, thunderstorm risk and visibility. If it is a town forecast, say what it misses.
2. Ask about the group today: illness, injuries, sleep, anyone dropping out, kit not yet ready.
3. Check the plan against both: does the turnaround time still work? Is any leg now unsafe (a ridge in high wind, a river crossing after heavy rain, a dry stretch in a heatwave)?
4. Decide: go as planned, go with named changes (earlier start, lower route, reversed direction, one fewer night), or no-go. Give the reasons and what would change the decision.
5. Update the trip plan and alarm time if anything changed, and remind them to send it.
6. Give three on-the-trail triggers to turn back for this specific trip.

Write it as Markdown with sections Forecast for your route, Group today, Decision (in bold), Changes to the plan, Turn-back triggers.

Stop. Tell the person to come back after the trip for a short debrief.
