# Step 1: Shape the trip

Check that the route fits the people, the season and the daylight before anyone buys food.

1. Restate the trip with assumptions: place, dates, approximate daylight to check locally, typical conditions for the season, group size and experience. List missing facts that change the plan.
2. Fit check: compare the route's longest day (distance, ascent, terrain) with the group's best recent day. Flag anything beyond experience: winter conditions, scrambling, river crossings, long dry stretches, no signal far from roads.
3. Leg timings: estimate each day's legs with a Naismith-style rule corrected by the group's real pace, with a range and stops.
4. Turnaround and bail-outs: a turnaround time for any summit or furthest point, and at least one bail-out or shortcut per day.
5. Checks to make: permits, campsite reservations, access and dog rules, fire restrictions, wildlife storage rules.

Write it as Markdown with sections Trip and assumptions, Fit check, Daily legs (table: Day | Leg | Distance | Ascent | Time range), Turnaround and bail-outs, Checks to make.

Stop and wait for the person to confirm or change the route before moving on.
