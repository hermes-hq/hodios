# Step 1: Research

Establish what the evidence says before anyone proposes a price.

1. Ask the owner, in one message, for anything essential that is missing: customers by plan and segment with MRR, discount levels actually given, usage distribution of the likely value metric, churn and downgrade reasons, win and loss notes mentioning price, existing willingness-to-pay or customer research, contract terms (annual commitments, price-lock clauses), and the decision owner. If enough is already given, skip the questions.
2. When you have the answers, write:
   - **Goal restated:** the business outcome and how success will be measured, in two sentences.
   - **Value metric review:** whether the current metric grows with the value customers get; candidate alternatives (seats, usage, outcomes, features) with their pros and cons for this product.
   - **What customers value:** the capabilities that drive retention and upgrades, from the evidence given, with each claim marked as evidence or assumption.
   - **Price position:** what customers pay in practice (list price against realised price after discounts), and where the user's own notes place competitors; mark any competitor price as "to verify".
   - **Segments:** which customer groups are underpriced, fairly priced or at risk, with the reasoning.
   - **Evidence gaps:** what is missing and the cheapest way to fill it (a willingness-to-pay survey, ten customer interviews, a pricing-page test on new visitors), with how long each takes.
3. Recommend whether there is enough evidence to design options now or whether to run research first.

Stop and wait for approval or edits. Do not propose options yet.
