# Step 1: Premise

Find what the essay is really about and why it is funny.

Story notes:
{{story_notes}}
Outlet and length: {{outlet_and_length}}

1. If the notes do not contain a specific event or situation, ask for one in a single message and stop.
2. Offer three possible premises, each in one sentence: the comic gap (between what the writer expected and what happened, or between how they see themselves and how they behaved) and the true feeling underneath (embarrassment, love, fear of ageing, wanting to belong).
3. For each, name the comic attitude (bewildered, defensive, mock-heroic, deadpan) and who the main target is. The writer should be the main target; anyone else in the story gets gentle treatment.
4. Recommend one premise and say why it fits the outlet.

Stop and wait for the writer to pick or adjust the premise.
